About

I am a generally curious and highly motivated astrophysicist interested in a broad range of observational and data-driven topics — particularly optical transient follow-up and applying modern machine learning techniques to gravitational-wave data.

Research

PhD · Present · LMU Munich (USM) · Prof. Daniel Gruen

O4 Gravitational-Wave Follow-up with Wendelstein Observatory

I am currently a PhD student in Prof. Daniel Gruen's group at the University Observatory Munich (USM / LMU). My main focus is an in-depth analysis of the gravitational-wave events we observed during the LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA O4 observing run with the 2m Fraunhofer Telescope at Wendelstein Observatory — mainly targeting binary black hole (BBH) mergers in search of potential electromagnetic counterparts flaring in active galactic nucleus (AGN) disks.

Master's Thesis · LMU Munich & Carnegie Mellon University · Prof. Antonella Palmese

Gravitational-Wave Populations & Machine Learning

For my Master's in the AI in Physics program at LMU Munich, I conducted my thesis research at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, supervised by Prof. Antonella Palmese. I worked on gravitational-wave population analysis and machine learning methods aimed at making population inference more stable and computationally efficient.

As part of this work, we developed a normalizing-flow emulator for gravitational-wave detection probability in O4 to reduce the Monte Carlo variance and cost of selection-effect estimation, alongside data-driven UMAP analyses of binary black hole subpopulations in gravitational-wave transient catalogs.

Target Selection & Data Reduction at Wendelstein

My involvement in optical follow-up of gravitational-wave events and other transients started with my Bachelor's thesis in Daniel Gruen's group at USM/LMU and continued in the years following. During that time, I worked on rapid target selection for follow-up campaigns and data reduction for observations taken at Wendelstein Observatory, including observations contributing to multi-facility studies of sub-solar mass / candidate superkilonova triggers such as AT2025ulz (S250818k) and S251112cm.

High-Energy & Optical Transient Follow-up

Beyond gravitational-wave triggers, I regularly contribute Wendelstein observations and data reduction to broader follow-up campaigns of high-energy and optical transients — including gamma-ray bursts (GRBs), Einstein Probe fast X-ray transients, and unusual supernovae — in collaborative studies led by Brendan O'Connor, Malte Busmann, and colleagues.
